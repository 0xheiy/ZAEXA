How ZAEXA works

Straight answers, including the ones that are not flattering.

What is ZAEXA?

A DEX aggregator on Base. When you enter an amount, it asks every pool it knows about on Base for a price, then builds the route that returns you the most — splitting your order across several pools when that pays better. It all happens in one transaction from your wallet.

Do you hold my money?

No. Your tokens leave your wallet, move through the pools, and come back to your wallet inside a single transaction. Nothing of yours sits in the contract between transactions. There is no account, no deposit, and no backend — the app runs entirely in your browser.

What am I approving, and what is this contract?

The contract is SwapExecutor, at on Base. Its source is verified on BaseScan and on Sourcify, so you can read exactly what it does before you trust it. By default the app asks you to approve only the amount of the swap you are making — not an unlimited allowance.

My wallet's security extension shows a red warning. Is something wrong?

Almost certainly not, and it is worth knowing what these tools are actually saying.

Pocket Universe shows "This is not covered — this is not insured." That is about their optional paid insurance product, not about your transaction. Their own simulation of a ZAEXA swap shows the correct amounts in and out, and flags nothing.

Rabby shows "Unknown Signature Type" and leaves the Protocol row empty. That means their contract database has no label for us yet — a new project, not a suspicious one. Rabby's balance-change simulation still runs, and it is correct.

What to check in any wallet, always: the amounts you send and receive, and that the request comes from zaexa.com.

Has the contract been audited?

No. The source is public and verified, it is covered by 67 tests, and it has been running on Base since 18 August 2026 — but no third party has audited it. That is a real difference, and you should size your trades accordingly.

What can the owner do?

Nothing to your funds. The contract holds nothing between transactions, and the owner cannot take tokens out of your wallet, cannot pause you, and cannot change the code — the contract is not upgradeable.

What the owner key can do, exactly: change the protocol fee, up to a 1% ceiling written into the contract that even the owner cannot raise; change which DEX routers the contract is allowed to call; withdraw tokens or ETH that end up stranded in the contract by accident; and hand ownership to another address, in two steps.

The router list is the one worth watching, since the contract will only ever call a router on it. Your protection is the minimum-output check: if a swap would return you less than the minimum shown before you signed, the whole transaction reverts and you keep your tokens.

What are the fees?

The protocol fee is currently 0%. You pay the pools' own fees, which are shown next to each venue, and network gas. Nothing else.

What do you collect about me?

Anonymous counts, and only four things: which view was opened, a few funnel steps, whether the screen is mobile or desktop, and a country code. Never your wallet address, the amount, the token, your IP, user agent, referrer or cookies — and no session or visitor identifier of any kind, so the counts can never be assembled back into a person. If your browser sends Global Privacy Control, nothing is recorded at all.

Why did my swap say "Simulation failed"?

Every swap is simulated against the live chain before anything is broadcast. If that simulation fails, nothing is sent and you pay no gas. It is the check that costs you nothing and saves you a failed transaction.

What is the Exit check?

Before you buy, it buys the token and sells it straight back inside one simulated transaction, then shows what that round trip actually costs. A token that cannot be sold back shows up right there. It is the honeypot test most interfaces do not run.
